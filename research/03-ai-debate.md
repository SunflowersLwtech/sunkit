## 1. Polanyi's Paradox in Economics and Labor

### Foundational Work: David Autor (2014)

**Paper:** "Polanyi's Paradox and the Shape of Employment Growth"
- NBER Working Paper No. 20485, September 2014
- Prepared for the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City's Jackson Hole symposium
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w20485
- Full PDF: https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/polanyis%20paradox%202014.pdf

**Core argument:** Named "Polanyi's Paradox" after Polanyi's dictum "We can know more than we can tell." Tasks requiring adaptability, common sense, and creativity remain immense challenges for automation. Machines both substitute for and complement human labor. Explains the polarization of employment into high-skill and low-skill jobs: routine middle-skill tasks are easily automated, tasks at both ends resist automation because they depend on tacit knowledge.

### Update: Autor et al. (2024)

**Paper:** "New Frontiers: The Origins and Content of New Work, 1940–2018"
- *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, Vol. 139, Issue 3, August 2024
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w30389

**Finding:** The majority of current employment is in job specialties introduced after 1940. The "race" between automation and new work creation is tightening. Demand-eroding effects of automation innovations have intensified while augmentation innovations have not kept pace.

## 2. Can LLMs Overcome Polanyi's Paradox?

### Optimistic View

**Source:** Jeremy Kahn, "Why AI might finally break Polanyi's Paradox"
- *Exponential View*, November 27, 2024
- URL: https://www.exponentialview.co/p/ai-polanyi-paradox

Modern AI can detect patterns in unstructured data (call transcripts, video recordings) and extract tacit knowledge without explicit documentation. Stanford/MIT study: AI coaching tools boosted customer service productivity by 14% overall and 34% for least experienced agents — transferring tacit knowledge of top performers to novices.

### Skeptical View

A 2023 NBER working paper argues that while generative AI extends beyond Polanyi's limits by automating creative outputs, LLMs often confabulate explanations post-hoc without genuine causal comprehension. ARC Prize competitions through 2025 confirm no system has approached human-level tacit proficiency.

### Business Perspective

**Source:** Paul Baier & Dr. John J. Sviokla, "Understand The Difference Between Explicit and Tacit Knowledge. AI Will Automate Both"
- *GAI Insights* (Substack), June 1, 2025
- URL: https://gaiinsights.substack.com/p/understand-the-difference-between

Introduces a novel category: **"emergent knowledge"** — insights and patterns that emerge when vast amounts of data are processed by AI systems. Predicts knowledge work of 5,000 employees will be handled by 4,000 humans + 10,000 AI agents.

## 3. Tacit Knowledge as Competitive Moat

**Source:** Teresa Tung & Philippe Roussiere, "Tacit Knowledge Is Your Next Competitive Moat"
- *California Management Review*, March 16, 2026
- URL: https://cmr.berkeley.edu/2026/03/tacit-knowledge-is-your-next-competitive-moat/

The real differentiator in the agentic AI age is not data or models but the tacit knowledge embedded in employees' judgment — "reasoning patterns, informal heuristics, situational awareness and nuanced interpretive skills."

**Five strategic actions:**
1. Map tacit knowledge flows
2. Codify without flattening richness
3. Build semantic layers
4. Enable human-AI collaboration
5. Position leaders as stewards of knowledge-rich AI ecosystems

**Warning:** Companies ignoring this face "brittle AI systems, inconsistent decisions, vanishing expertise."

## 4. Embodied Cognition and AI Limitations

**Sources:**
- "A Review of Embodied AI and the Road Ahead" (arXiv, May 2025) — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14235
- "Embodied AI: From LLMs to World Models" (Frontiers in Robotics and AI, 2025)

**Findings:** Current embodied AI systems based on foundation models provide "third-person (impersonal) experience with a low degree of embodied cognitive interaction." Experiments on 14 state-of-the-art multimodal LLMs reveal fundamental limitations:
- Difficulties with implicit instruction comprehension
- Spatiotemporal reasoning gaps
- Cross-scenario planning failures
- Fine-grained affordance understanding lacking
- Execution failure diagnosis problematic

Self-reflection intelligence in dynamic, uncertain environments remains a key unsolved challenge.

## 6. Medical/Clinical Applications

### Tacit Knowledge in Medical Education (2026)
**Source:** Papadimos et al., "Insights From Michael Polanyi: Tacit Knowledge and Its Critical Importance in Medical Education"
- *Cureus*, January 2026
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12927663/

Tacit knowledge requires direct mentorship — learners cannot replicate complex tasks like patient diagnosis without physical presence. COVID-19 and AI adoption threaten bedside teaching. Advanced simulation with AI should supplement, not replace, human clinical mentorship.

### Evidence-Based Medicine and Tacit Knowledge
**Source:** Greenhalgh, "Tacit knowledge as the unifying factor in evidence based medicine and clinical judgement"
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1475611/

Healthcare professionals employ experiential, often non-verbal understanding. Connects to "non-analytical reasoning" and "dual process of reasoning."

### Management Reasoning vs. AI Diagnosis
**Source:** "Elevating Management Reasoning to Preserve Professional Identity in the AI Era"
- *Journal of General Internal Medicine*, 2026
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-026-10182-3

AI approaches expert-level diagnostic reasoning, but **"management reasoning"** — translating diagnoses into personalized care — remains distinctly human. Requires integrating human values, navigating system constraints, building therapeutic relationships.

## 9. Prompt Engineering as Tacit Knowledge

### Academic Framework
**Source:** Federiakin et al., "Prompt engineering as a new 21st century skill"
- *Frontiers in Education*, 2024
- URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2024.1366434/full

Prompt engineering is a distinct competency, not reducible to general communication skills. Tacit elements include meta-awareness, ambiguity tolerance, iterative refinement, and the dual cognitive role of being both task processor and quality supervisor. Skills are perishable — what works changes as models evolve.

### Craft vs. Science
**Source:** Shelly Palmer, "Mastering the Art of Prompt Engineering"
- November 2024
- URL: https://shellypalmer.com/2024/11/mastering-the-art-of-prompt-engineering/

Early practice was "more craft than science." Shifting from "prompt crafting" (artisanal) toward "AI system design" (systematic). Mirrors how tacit skills get partially codified through frameworks.

### Career Market
**Source:** "Prompt Engineer: Analyzing Skill Requirements in the AI Job Market"
- arXiv, 2025
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00058v1

Organizations weaving prompt skills into existing roles rather than creating standalone positions. Prompt engineering's tacit component makes it resistant to becoming a standalone discipline — remains embedded in domain expertise.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

1. **The paradox is weakening but not broken.** LLMs approximate some tacit knowledge forms (sales, customer service patterns), but genuine causal comprehension, embodied skill, and contextual judgment remain beyond reach.

2. **A new knowledge category is emerging.** "Emergent knowledge" — insights that only arise when AI processes data at scale — suggests AI creates a new epistemic category, not just captures existing tacit knowledge.

3. **The business case is urgent.** Baby Boomer retirements + the CMR piece + Interloom's growth = real market for tacit knowledge capture before expertise walks out the door.

4. **Embodiment remains the hard problem.** The gap between language-based AI and situated, embodied intelligence is where Dreyfus's 50-year-old critique still bites hardest. VR/AR is the most promising bridge but supplements rather than replaces mentorship.

5. **Prompt engineering exemplifies the recursion.** The skill of working with AI is itself partly tacit — dependent on intuition, iteration, and domain knowledge — creating a recursive loop where tacit knowledge is needed to extract tacit knowledge.
