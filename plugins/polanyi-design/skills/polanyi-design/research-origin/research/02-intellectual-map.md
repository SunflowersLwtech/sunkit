# Tacit Knowledge: Intellectual Map Around Michael Polanyi

---

## 1. Direct Intellectual Lineage

### Michael Polanyi (1891–1976)
**Core concepts:** Tacit knowing (from-to structure), subsidiary vs. focal awareness, indwelling, emergence, personal knowledge, fiduciary framework.

### Marjorie Grene (1910–2009)
**Role:** Philosopher of biology, Polanyi's closest philosophical collaborator and interpreter.
**Key works:** *The Knower and the Known* (1966), *A Philosophical Testament* (1995), edited *Knowing and Being* (1969)
**Connection:** Bridge between Polanyi and continental philosophy (Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty). Helped articulate how Polanyi's epistemology relates to philosophy of biology and the mind-body problem.

### Harry Prosch (1917–2005)
**Key works:** *Meaning* (1975, with Polanyi), *Michael Polanyi: A Critical Exposition* (1986)
**Connection:** Completed *Meaning* after Polanyi's health declined. Scholarly debate about how much reflects Prosch's interpretive overlay.

### The Polanyi Society (Founded 1970s)
- **Phil Mullins** — Long-time editor of *Tradition and Discovery*, central figure in Polanyi scholarship
- **Walter Gulick** — Polanyi's relevance to religious epistemology and philosophy of meaning
- **Dale Cannon** — Polanyi and philosophy of religion
- **Andy Sanders** — *Post-Critical Epistemology* (1988), situating Polanyi vs critical rationalism
- **Esther Meek** — *Longing to Know* (2003), *Loving to Know* (2011), developed "covenant epistemology"
- **Mark Mitchell** — *The Art of Knowing* (2006), accessible intellectual biography

## 2. Knowledge Management School

### Ikujiro Nonaka (1935–2023) & Hirotaka Takeuchi (b. 1946)
**Key works:** *The Knowledge-Creating Company* (1995), "The Concept of 'Ba'" (1998), *Managing Flow* (2008)

**SECI Model:**
- **Socialization** (tacit → tacit): shared experience, apprenticeship
- **Externalization** (tacit → explicit): articulation via metaphor, analogy, models
- **Combination** (explicit → explicit): systematizing, combining documents/data
- **Internalization** (explicit → tacit): learning by doing, embodiment

**Ba:** Shared context/space (physical, virtual, mental) where knowledge creation occurs. Derived from Kitaro Nishida.

**⚠️ Critical note:** Nonaka arguably *distorted* Polanyi. Polanyi saw tacit knowledge as a *dimension* of all knowing, not a separate *type* that can be "converted" to explicit. The SECI model's "externalization" is philosophically problematic from a strict Polanyian perspective. Collins and others have criticized this.

### Harry Collins (b. 1943)
**Key works:** *Tacit and Explicit Knowledge* (2010), *Artifictional Intelligence* (2018)

**Taxonomy of tacit knowledge:**
1. **Relational tacit knowledge (RTK):** Tacit only contingently — could in principle be made explicit
2. **Somatic tacit knowledge (STK):** Embodied in the body (riding a bicycle). Body-dependent.
3. **Collective tacit knowledge (CTK):** Embedded in social practices. *The strong form* — inherently resistant to explicitation because it depends on socialization into a community.

**Connection:** Both extends and critiques Polanyi. Argues Polanyi conflated different *kinds* of tacitness. CTK connects to Wittgenstein's "forms of life."

### Karl-Erik Sveiby (b. 1946)
**Key works:** *The New Organizational Wealth* (1997)
**Connection:** Organizational value lies in intangible, knowledge-based assets — particularly tacit knowledge in people and relationships.

## 3. Philosophical Predecessors and Parallels

### Gestalt Psychology (Wertheimer, Köhler, Koffka)
**Connection:** **Foundational** to Polanyi. His from-to structure is a generalized Gestalt theory of cognition. We perceive a face by attending *from* features *to* the integrated whole. Polanyi generalized this beyond perception to all epistemology.
**Read:** Köhler's *Gestalt Psychology* (1929), then *The Tacit Dimension* chs. 1–4.

### Ludwig Wittgenstein (1889–1951)
**Key works:** *Philosophical Investigations* (1953), *On Certainty* (1969)
**Connection:** Parallel problems, largely independent. Both attacked propositional/explicit knowledge as exhaustive. Wittgenstein's rule-following problem (PI §§198-202) parallels Polanyi's argument that tacit knowledge underlies all explicit knowledge. "Forms of life" resonates with Collins's collective tacit knowledge.
**Difference:** Wittgenstein anti-systematic, refused to build a theory. Polanyi explicitly constructed one.

### Gilbert Ryle (1900–1976)
**Key work:** *The Concept of Mind* (1949)
**Connection:** "Knowing how" vs. "knowing that." Both reject practice as merely applied theory. But Ryle is narrower and more linguistic/behavioral. Polanyi's tacit knowledge is richer (from-to structure, body, indwelling, emergence).
**Note:** Stanley & Williamson (2001) controversially argued knowing-how *is* knowing-that, which would undermine Ryle but not Polanyi.

### Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)
**Key work:** *Being and Time* (1927)
**Connection:** **Extraordinarily close parallels.** Polanyi's subsidiary awareness maps onto Heidegger's ready-to-hand (Zuhandenheit). Both argue theoretical knowledge is parasitic on practical engagement. Polanyi's "indwelling" ≈ Heidegger's being-in-the-world.
**Difference:** Heidegger does ontology; Polanyi does epistemology.
**Read:** *Being and Time* §§15-18; Dreyfus's commentary as bridge.

### Maurice Merleau-Ponty (1908–1961)
**Key works:** *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945), *The Visible and the Invisible* (1964)
**Connection:** Provides the philosophical anthropology Polanyi implies. The blind man's cane becoming part of his body-schema = Polanyi's indwelling. Both see knowing as irreducibly embodied.
**Read:** *Phenomenology of Perception*, Part I.

### Hubert Dreyfus (1929–2017) & Stuart Dreyfus (b. 1931)
**Key works:** *What Computers Can't Do* (1972/1992), *Mind Over Machine* (1986), *Being-in-the-World* (1991)

**Dreyfus Skill Acquisition Model (5 stages):**
1. **Novice:** Follows context-free rules
2. **Advanced beginner:** Recognizes situational elements
3. **Competent:** Conscious deliberation, goal-setting
4. **Proficient:** Intuitive situation recognition, deliberate response
5. **Expert:** Intuitive recognition *and* response — no conscious deliberation

**Connection:** Developmental account of explicit → tacit through practice. At expert level, knowledge is fully embodied. AI critique was deeply Polanyian/Heideggerian.
**⚠️ Update:** Deep learning complicates the Dreyfus critique — modern AI uses pattern recognition in ways that are themselves somewhat "tacit." Live debate.

## 4. Contemporary Applications

### Jean Lave (b. 1939) & Etienne Wenger (1952–2023)
**Key works:** *Situated Learning* (1991), *Communities of Practice* (1998)
**Connection:** Operationalizes tacit knowledge transfer. Learning through "legitimate peripheral participation" — gradually moving from periphery to center. Knowledge is in practices, not heads.

### Donald Schön (1930–1997)
**Key works:** *The Reflective Practitioner* (1983), *Educating the Reflective Practitioner* (1987)
**Connection:** "Knowing-in-action" = Polanyian tacit knowledge in professional context. "Reflection-in-action" = making the tacit somewhat explicit when practice breaks down. Enormously influential in education, nursing, architecture.

### Bent Flyvbjerg (b. 1952)
**Key works:** *Making Social Science Matter* (2001), *How Big Things Get Done* (2023)
**Connection:** Phronesis (Aristotelian practical wisdom) as the model for social science. Expert practical judgment = tacit knowledge that resists formalization. Drew on Dreyfus model directly.

### Matthew Crawford (b. 1965)
**Key works:** *Shop Class as Soulcraft* (2009), *The World Beyond Your Head* (2015), *Why We Drive* (2020)
**Connection:** Motorcycle mechanic with PhD. Manual/craft work involves sophisticated tacit knowing. Draws on Polanyi, Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty. The mechanic diagnosing an engine by sound = tacit knowledge in action.

### Richard Sennett (b. 1943)
**Key works:** *The Craftsman* (2008), *Together* (2012), *Building and Dwelling* (2018)
**Connection:** Craftsmanship as continuous dialogue between hand and head. The craftsman's knowledge is in the hands, the trained eye, the feel for materials.

## 5. AI-Era Thinkers

### David Autor (b. 1967)
**Key paper:** "Polanyi's Paradox and the Shape of Employment Growth" (NBER, 2014)
**Concept:** Named "Polanyi's Paradox" — many tasks humans perform routinely involve tacit knowledge difficult to automate.
**Update (2024):** Deep learning is beginning to erode the paradox. AI might restore the middle class by giving workers with less formal training access to expert-level decision support.

### Erik Brynjolfsson (b. 1962)
**Key works:** *The Second Machine Age* (2014), "Generative AI at Work" (2023)
**Connection:** His 2023 paper showed AI tools disproportionately helped less-skilled workers — essentially encoding expert tacit knowledge into AI systems novices could use.

### Yann LeCun (b. 1960)
**Key idea:** "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (2022). Insistence on "world models" and JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture).
**Connection:** Arguably the most Polanyian program in AI. LLMs lack internal representations of how the physical world works — need embodied engagement, not just symbolic processing. His LLM critique echoes Polanyi and Collins on collective tacit knowledge.

### Other Notable Voices
- **Alison Gopnik** — Children's learning, "theory theory," critical of LLM understanding
- **Gary Marcus** — Persistent critic of deep learning's brittleness, echoes Dreyfus
- **Melanie Mitchell** — *AI: A Guide for Thinking Humans* (2019)
- **Shannon Vallor** — *Technology and the Virtues* (2016), AI ethics meets virtue epistemology
- **Matt Beane** — *The Skill Code* (2024), how AI disrupts apprenticeship and tacit skill transfer
